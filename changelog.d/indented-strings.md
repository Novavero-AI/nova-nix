- **Indented strings strip exactly what upstream strips: whitespace-only lines, the opener's own line, and spaces but never tabs.** The stripping was approximate in four ways. A string with no measured line (blank lines and spaces only) kept every space, because the minimum indent started at zero when nothing contributed; upstream starts it at a sentinel, so such a string loses every leading space, and `''` followed by a blank line and an indented closer is `"\n"`, not the eight spaces of the closer. A tab counted as indentation and was stripped; upstream measures only spaces, so a tab fixes the minimum at its column and survives into the value. The closing `''`'s own indentation survived as a trailing line of spaces; upstream removes a final line of spaces from the last part after stripping. And the opener's trailing spaces survived as a line of their own, because only a bare newline after `''` was dropped; upstream's lexer consumes `''`, any spaces and one newline as the opener, so content starts on the next line. The same transcription corrects how escapes meet stripping: upstream's scan skips an escape (it ends a line's leading whitespace without being measured) while its strip runs over escapes as over any literal, so the spaces after an escaped newline are stripped but never measured, and `''  a''\n  b''` is `"a\nb"` where it was `"a\n  b"`. Every rule is `ParserState::stripIndentation` and the `IND_STRING_OPEN` lexer rule at Nix 2.24.9, unchanged through 2.33.2, and every case was checked against nix-instantiate 2.33.2. nixpkgs concatenates `+ ''`, a blank line, `''` into shell hooks constantly, and clang's drvPath differed from upstream's for that reason alone. (#220)
- **`Nix.Expr.Types.StringPart` has a new constructor, `StrEsc`, and `Nix.Eval.StringInterp.stripIndentedChunks` takes a list of the new `StringChunk` type.** An indented-string escape (`'''`, `''$`, `''\n`) was parsed as a constant interpolation, which made it indistinguishable from `${"..."}` by the time indentation was stripped, and upstream strips the two differently. `StrEsc` carries the resolved text as a part of its own, so exhaustive matches over `StringPart` need the case. `StringChunk` (`ChunkLit`, `ChunkEsc`, `ChunkInterp` with its context) replaces the `(Bool, ByteString, StringContext)` triple so an evaluated part keeps its kind, and `concatChunks` joins chunks without stripping, as a double-quoted string does.
