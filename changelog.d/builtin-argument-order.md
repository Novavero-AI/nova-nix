- **A builtin checks each argument before it forces the next, as upstream's primops do, so of two bad arguments the earlier one's refusal is the error: `builtins.hasAttr 1 (throw "x")` fails with `expected a string but found an integer: 1`, and `builtins.storePath` under pure evaluation is refused before its argument is forced.** nova-nix forced every argument of these builtins as it was applied and checked them only once all were there, so a later argument that failed to evaluate hid an earlier one of the wrong type. Upstream's primops (primops.cc and context.cc, the same at 2.24.9, 2.34.9 and 2.36.0) force and check their arguments one at a time, first to last: `hasAttr`, `getAttr`, `catAttrs`, `match`, `split`, `compareVersions`, `toFile`, `concatStringsSep`, `appendContext`, `removeAttrs`, `intersectAttrs` and `bitAnd`, `bitOr` and `bitXor` check the first before they force the second; `hashString` and `hashFile` refuse an algorithm they do not know before they force what they hash; `substring` checks its start, refusing a negative one with `negative start position in 'substring'`, before it forces the length, and the length before the string; `replaceStrings` compares the lengths of its two lists, refusing a difference in upstream's words, and forces every string it replaces, before it forces the string it works on; `findFile` reads every entry, and realises each one's context, before it forces the name; and `concatMap`, `any`, `all`, `partition`, `groupBy` and `foldl'` force their function with upstream's `forceFunction`, which takes a lambda, a builtin or a set with `__functor`, before they force their list, so `builtins.any 1 [ ]` fails with `expected a function but found an integer: 1` where it was `false`. A first argument of the wrong type is refused with upstream's `expected ... but found ...` where nova-nix had words of its own. `findFile` reads an entry as upstream does: its `prefix` may be absent, and then is empty, its `path` is coerced as a string, so a set with `outPath` is a path, and one without a `path` is refused with `attribute 'path' missing`. A builtin applied to only some of its arguments still forces each as it is applied, where upstream forces none of them until the last.
