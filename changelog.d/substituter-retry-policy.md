- **The substituter retries a narinfo or NAR download under upstream's transfer-layer policy, the one `builtin:fetchurl` already used.** Upstream serves every download through one `FileTransfer` (`libstore/filetransfer.cc` at 2.24.9) with one backoff and one table of the failures that are never retried, and the substituter kept an older policy of its own. A narinfo fetch was not retried at all, so a single timeout, reset or 5xx on the narinfo cost the path its substitution and, with no other cache holding it, a local build; it now gets upstream's five attempts. A NAR download retried every 5xx and every failure the HTTP client raised, so a 501, 505 or 511 and a redirect loop spent five attempts on an answer that cannot change; they now stop at once, as upstream's never-retried statuses and curl results do, while a 4xx other than 408 and 429 stops at once as before. The delays between attempts become upstream's 250 ms doubling with jitter on the exponent, where the NAR retry waited 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 seconds, and each retry is announced on stderr with upstream's `; retrying in N ms` suffix. A narinfo answered 404, 410, 401, 403 or 407 is a miss and is never retried, where only a 404 was a miss and the other four were substitution errors: upstream's transfer layer calls the first two `NotFound` and the rest `Forbidden`, and its binary cache store reads both as the file not being in that cache, since an S3 bucket that cannot be listed answers a missing key with 403. A NAR the narinfo named and the cache answers the same way stops after one request, as before and as upstream's `SubstituteGone` does. An unreachable cache therefore costs each narinfo the full retry budget before the scan moves on (the delays alone come to four to five seconds), as it does upstream, which disables a failing cache for a minute only when `fallback` is set. A connection reset in the middle of a body is now a transport failure, retried like any other, in `builtin:fetchurl` too: the HTTP client wraps what fails while a response is opened but hands the body reader over unwrapped, so a reset mid-body arrived as a bare `IOException` and was taken for a failure writing the output, where curl's receive errors are transient upstream. `bcFetchRetry` governs the substituter's requests as well as `builtin:fetchurl`'s, as upstream's one `download-attempts` setting governs every transfer.
