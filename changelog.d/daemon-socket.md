- **`nova-nix daemon` serves the store to clients over a Unix socket, in upstream Nix's worker protocol, on Linux and macOS.** It listens where upstream's daemon does, at `$NIX_DAEMON_SOCKET_PATH`, else `daemon-socket/socket` in `$NIX_STATE_DIR` or `/nix/var/nix`, or where `--socket-path` says, on a socket every user may write, as upstream's is, so the directory it is in decides who reaches it. It admits and trusts each client as upstream's daemon does, by `trusted-users` and `allowed-users`, which name users, groups as `@GROUP` and everyone as `*`, refusing a member of `build-users-group`, which defaults to `nixbld` for a daemon run as root; `--force-trusted` and `--force-untrusted` decide without asking who a client is, as upstream's flags do. A client the daemon does not trust is limited as upstream limits one: it may not repair, its settings are held to those upstream lets it set, its substituters to `trusted-substituters` and those the daemon uses, and it is shown roots without what holds them. Each connection is served on a thread of its own, with temporary roots of its own that go with it, and the daemon holds the store open while it runs, so a collection waits for it to stop. It answers queries of the store (which paths are valid, their info, referrers and derivers, a derivation's outputs, the roots, a path's NAR) and keeps temporary and indirect roots; a client asking it to build, substitute, add paths, add signatures or collect garbage is told it does not do so yet, since importing a client's paths safely and collecting with its roots need the store to check what it imports and to read upstream's roots. A path too long for a socket address is refused, where upstream binds one from its directory, and on macOS a group's members include those the directory counts, as upstream's do. Windows has no daemon yet, and the command says so. `trusted-users`, `allowed-users`, `build-users-group` and `trusted-substituters` (and its older name `trusted-binary-caches`) are read from `nix.conf`.
- **Library API:** `Nix.Daemon.Listen` listens on a socket and serves each admitted client on its own thread (`openListener`, `serveListener`), `Nix.Daemon.Auth` decides admission over a user database it is given (`admitPeer`), and `Nix.Daemon.LocalStore` serves the local store to each connection (`serveLocal`). `NixConfig` has `ncTrustedUsers`, `ncAllowedUsers`, `ncTrustedSubstituters` and `ncBuildUsersGroup`, and `Nix.Store.GC` exports `addIndirectRoot`. The library depends on `network`.
