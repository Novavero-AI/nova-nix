- **Operators bind as upstream's grammar declares them: `-5 ? a` is `(-5) ? a`, which is `false`, `++` binds tighter than `*` and `+`, `a ? b ? c` is `(a ? b) ? c`, and `!` may stand as the operand of a tighter operator, as in `1 + !x`.** Upstream's grammar (parser.y, the same at 2.24.9, 2.34.9 and 2.36.0) binds, loosest first, `->`, `||`, `&&`, `==` and `!=`, the comparisons, `//`, `!`, `+` and `-`, `*` and `/`, `++`, `?`, and unary `-`, and a prefix operator's operand there runs as far as the operators tighter than it, so `a + !b + c` is `a + !(b + c)`. nova-nix bound unary `-` looser than `?`, so `-5 ? a` failed on negating a Boolean where upstream answers `false`; bound `++` looser than `+`, so `1 * [ ] ++ throw "c"` failed on the product where upstream reaches the throw; refused a second `?`; and refused `!` after a tighter operator, so a file holding `let x = 1 + !true; in 2` did not parse where upstream evaluates it to `2`. `a == b == c` and `a < b > c` are still refused, at the second operator, as upstream refuses them.
